## 01. The Infrastructure Underneath Your Agent: What Providers Are Rebuilding and Why It Matters

Recent accounts place coding agents inside source-control infrastructure, review workflows, and permission systems. The useful distinction for small teams is which changes are demonstrated, which are still being built, and which oversight responsibilities remain theirs—not whether large-provider activity figures predict their own productivity.

## 02. Green Lights, Empty Boxes: What the Evidence Actually Shows About AI Agent Verification

Different verification tools answer different questions. Benchmark labels, operational monitoring, work-product checks, and agent decision notes should not be treated as interchangeable proof of success. The sources support using them as complementary signals, with explicit limits on what each can establish.

## 03. A Small-Team Adoption Plan: Verify One Task, Budget the Controls, Watch the Roadmap

Small teams can evaluate agent adoption one bounded workflow at a time: identify the person responsible for the result, choose proportionate checks, and distinguish available tools from promised infrastructure. The sources reveal several kinds of implementation cost, but do not establish that every team needs an enterprise stack or that adoption costs are rising faster than accessibility.
